## How to read this document

Three things will mislead you if you skim.

1. **Proportions are not counts.** The registry publishes leading sites as *percentages*.
   The registry itself grew 31% over the window. A site whose percentage *fell* can have a
   *rising* absolute count. Wherever it matters I give both, and mark derived counts with `~`.
2. **This is a hospital registry, not an incidence registry.** HBCR counts who *came to MCC*.
   A rise can mean more disease, or better referral, or a new machine that pulls cases in.
   Population incidence lives in the separate MPBCR reports (§5), not here.
3. **Causes in §4 are hypotheses, explicitly labelled.** MCC's reports state no causes. Every
   explanation in that section is mine and is unverified.

### What moved most

- **Follow-up visits +66%** and **total visits +59%**, against new registrations +27%. The
  centre is accumulating *survivors* faster than it is acquiring new patients. Follow-up is
  now **14× the new-registration count** (113,791 vs 8,214).
- **Day chemotherapy +68%** — faster than new cases. More patients, more lines of therapy, or
  longer treatment durations.
- **Major surgeries doubled** (1,205 → 2,396).
- **2020-21 is the COVID dent**: follow-up fell 15%, day chemo fell 8.5%, total visits fell
  14% — yet *new cancer cases rose* 4,491 → 5,384. MCC also ran **123,000 RT-PCR and 5,200
  antigen COVID tests** that year, the only year such a line appears.
- **Beds: 353 operational** as of 2024-25.

### Radiotherapy — the exception that did not grow

| Measure | 2019-20 | 2020-21 | 2021-22 | 2022-23 | 2023-24 | 2024-25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Curative RT | 957 | 994 | 1,194 | 1,305 | 1,199 | 1,163 |
| Palliative RT | 1,076 | 958 | 1,221 | 1,292 | 1,043 | 1,046 |
| Brachytherapy | 478 | 277 | 663 | 491 | 406 | 577 |
| 4DCT simulation | 1,699 | 2,344 | 2,529 | 2,548 | 2,248 | **2,739** |

Radiotherapy **peaked in 2022-23 and has since fallen back**, while surgery and chemotherapy
climbed steeply. Curative RT in 2024-25 (1,163) is below 2021-22 (1,194). This is the single
most interesting divergence in the volume data and the reports offer no explanation.

## 4. Possible reasons — HYPOTHESES, NOT FINDINGS

> MCC's reports state **no causes anywhere**. Everything in this section is my interpretation,
> unverified, and should be challenged before it is repeated. I have tried to give the
> ascertainment explanation equal weight to the biological one in every case, because a
> hospital registry cannot distinguish them.

### Female thyroid, 3.3% → 8.6% in two years (~85 → 253 cases)

The steepest move in the dataset, and the most likely to be **artefact rather than disease**.

- **Overdiagnosis via imaging access (most likely).** Ultrasound volume rose 32% and
  mammography 18% over the overlapping period. Thyroid cancer has the classic overdiagnosis
  signature worldwide: incidence tracks ultrasound availability, mortality does not move.
  A near-tripling in two years is not biologically plausible for a real incidence shift.
- **Referral capture.** As MCC's reputation and capability grew, thyroid cases previously
  managed in general surgery elsewhere may now route here.
- **A classification or coding change** in the registry. Cannot be excluded from the reports.
- **Counter-consideration:** Kerala genuinely reports high thyroid cancer rates nationally.
  So some of this may be real. The reports cannot separate the two.
- **Test:** check whether thyroid *mortality* or *stage at presentation* moved. If stage
  shifted toward early/incidental, it is overdiagnosis.

### Colorectal, rising 29-71% against an 11% registry growth

- **Demographic transition (likely contributor).** Kerala has India's oldest population
  profile and the highest life expectancy. Colorectal incidence is strongly age-dependent.
- **Dietary and lifestyle transition.** Rising red/processed meat, refined carbohydrate,
  obesity and sedentary behaviour — the standard explanation for CRC rise across urbanising
  Asia. The registry department runs a "Lifestyle and Cancer" project, suggesting MCC itself
  takes this seriously, but publishes no result in these reports.
- **Ascertainment from new capability (likely co-contributor).** EUS/EBUS (2022), 3D
  laparoscopy (2023), second cryostat (2023), robotic surgery (2024). A centre that becomes
  better at GI cancer attracts GI cancer. **This cannot be separated from true incidence in
  a hospital registry.**
- **Test:** compare against the MPBCR (population) colorectal trend for Kannur/Kasaragod. If
  the population registry shows the same slope, it is real incidence; if only the hospital
  registry rises, it is referral.

### Male lung — share falling, count flat

- Kerala has sustained tobacco control and one of India's lower smoking prevalences, and the
  long-run proportional decline across **all** tobacco-related sites (lung, mouth, tongue,
  larynx, oesophagus) is consistent with that.
- But the flat absolute count says the burden has not shrunk — the denominator grew.
- Male tongue fell hardest in absolute terms (~158 → ~125, −21%), which is the cleanest
  tobacco/areca signal in the data.

### Male liver, 3.1% → 5.9% (~132 → ~185, +40%)

- Candidate drivers: alcohol-related liver disease (Kerala has high per-capita consumption),
  NAFLD/metabolic liver disease rising with diabetes prevalence, chronic hepatitis B/C.
- Also plausibly **detection**: interventional radiology started 2019, 64-slice fluoro-CT
  2020, PET/CT 2021. More imaging finds more liver lesions.
- Unresolved and worth a question — this is the second-fastest riser after thyroid.

### Female cervix, 7.3% → 5.8% (~191 → ~170, −11% absolute)

- The one unambiguously *good* trend. Consistent with screening uptake and awareness; Kerala
  has strong community-health infrastructure, and MCC runs community oncology programmes and
  a cervix patterns-of-care study.
- Too early to attribute anything to HPV vaccination at these ages.

### Follow-up visits +66% while new registrations rose 27%

- **Survivorship accumulation.** Patients are living longer and returning. This is the
  signature of improving outcomes, not of rising disease.
- Operationally it means the **surveillance burden is growing twice as fast as the intake
  burden** — which is exactly the setting where an order-time decision support check pays
  off, because the clinician is seeing far more returning patients per new one.

### Radiotherapy flat-to-falling while surgery and chemo climbed

- Possible shift in modality mix toward **surgery-first** (robotic 2024, 3D laparoscopy 2023)
  and **systemic therapy** (immunotherapy since 2018, CAR-T 2024).
- Possible capacity ceiling: the linear accelerators are from 2010/2011/2015 and the cobalt
  unit from 2019. Machine capacity, not demand, may be the binding constraint.
- The reports do not say. **A genuinely open question.**

## 5. Region — where the patients come from

### Catchment, as MCC states it

- Core: **Kannur, Kasaragod** districts and **Mahe** (Puducherry UT enclave). These are the
  three districts of the original 2014 Malabar Population Based Cancer Registry.
- **Expanded 2018** to five more Kerala districts: **Kozhikode, Malappuram, Wayanad, Thrissur,
  Palakkad** — eight districts total under MPBCR surveillance.
- Wikipedia (secondary, weaker) adds neighbouring Karnataka, Tamil Nadu.
- MCC describes itself as "a tertiary cancer centre in **northern Kerala**" and, in one 2017-18
  conference abstract, as serving a **tribal** population (a poster on "Cancer patterns, risk
  habits, treatment, and follow up rate of tribal at a tertiary cancer care centre in
  Northern Kerala").

### ⚠️ No district-wise patient breakdown is published

I grepped all six reports for district-wise or residence-wise distribution of MCC's *patients*.
**There is none.** The annual reports never state what share of MCC's caseload comes from
Kannur vs Kasaragod vs elsewhere. That is a real gap and it has to be asked for directly.

What *is* published is population-level incidence by district, from MPBCR.

### Population registry, MPBCR 2016 — leading sites by district (% of that district's cases)

**Kannur district, males** — Lung 23.0, Stomach 7.6, NHL 5.6, Prostate 5.0, Rectum 4.7,
Larynx 4.6, Mouth 4.3, Tongue 3.8, Liver 3.5, Oesophagus 3.3

**Kannur district, females** — Breast 28.8, Ovary 6.35, Cervix 6.22, Thyroid 4.9, Lung 4.6,
NHL 3.9, Stomach 3.6, **Colon 3.5**, Mouth 3.4, Rectum 3.4

**Kasaragod district, males** — Lung 19.6, **Mouth 10.4**, Stomach 7.5, Tongue 6.2,
**Oropharynx 4.6**, NHL 4.1, Larynx 3.9, Prostate 3.7, Liver 3.7, Oesophagus 3.1

### The district contrast worth noting

**Kasaragod men have 2.4× the oral cancer share of Kannur men (10.4% vs 4.3%)**, plus
oropharynx in the top ten where Kannur has none, and higher tongue (6.2 vs 3.8).

**Hypothesis (mine, unverified):** differing tobacco and areca-nut/betel-quid habits between
the two districts. Kasaragod borders Karnataka and has distinct demographic and linguistic
communities. This is a well-described pattern in South Indian oral cancer epidemiology but I
have **not** verified it for these specific districts.

**Implication for Anukriti:** the catchment is **not homogeneous**. A population-aware claim
made for "Malabar" averages over districts that differ by a factor of two on a major site.
If a DPYD allele-frequency argument is ever made at district resolution, this heterogeneity
has to be acknowledged rather than averaged away.

> **Caveat:** MPBCR 2016 is a decade old. Five MPBCR reports exist (2014-2018) and are
> published separately from the annual reports. They were not retrieved for this note and are
> the correct source for current district-level incidence.

## 7. Summary — what actually changed, 2019 to 2025

**Rising**
- Follow-up/survivorship burden (+66%) — fastest-growing thing in the institution
- Day chemotherapy (+68%)
- Major surgery (+99%)
- Female thyroid (+198% in count, 2020→2022) — probably substantially overdiagnosis
- **Colorectal (+29% to +71%, 2020→2022)** — rising far faster than the registry
- Male liver (+40%)
- Female corpus uteri (+37%)

**Flat**
- Male lung (count flat, share falling — still the single largest male site)
- Female breast (still ~29-31% of all female cancers throughout)
- Radiotherapy — peaked 2022-23, now below its 2021-22 level
- PET/CT

**Falling**
- Female cervix (−11% in count) — the clearest genuine good-news trend
- Male tongue (−21%), mouth, larynx, oesophagus — the tobacco/areca cluster, proportionally
- SPECT/CT (−76%)

**Unknown and worth asking**
- Why radiotherapy stalled while surgery and chemotherapy grew
- What share of patients come from which district (never published)
- Whether the colorectal rise is incidence or ascertainment (needs the MPBCR series)
- Whether 2024-25 DPYD testing was 19 or 276

## ⚠️ Corrections to the analysis above

**1. The colorectal rise did not continue.** Colorectal was ~305-406 cases in 2020 and ~522
in 2022 (derived). The exact 2024 figure is **509**. So the step up happened between 2020 and
2022 and has since been flat. The "+29% to +71%" finding stands *for that window* and must
not be stated as an ongoing trend.

**2. Male lung is now falling in absolute count, not just in share.** 544 (2020) → 533 (2022)
→ 460 (2023) → 462 (2024). The earlier "flat count" reading was correct through 2022 and is
now superseded.

**3. Female thyroid plateaued.** 253 (2022) → 248 (2023) → 274 (2024). The surge was a
two-year step between 2020 and 2022, not a continuing climb. A step-change is what a
detection or coding shift looks like, so this **strengthens** the overdiagnosis hypothesis.

**4. The registry total has stopped growing.** 6,174 (2021) → 6,059 (2022) → 5,622 (2023) →
5,905 (2024). The 2021 peak has not been beaten.

**5. District-wise patient distribution IS published** — in the HBCR reports, not the annual
reports. The claim above that it is nowhere published was wrong for this source.

## 🔴 The real fluoropyrimidine denominator

The HBCR reports carry a **treatment-modality table** the annual reports do not. It covers
patients treated at MCC, excluding those previously treated elsewhere — so it is a subset of
registrations, and it is the honest denominator.

Chemotherapy-containing = C-Therapy + Surgery&Chemo + Radio&Chemo + Surgery&Radio&Chemo.

| Site | Treated at MCC | Chemo-containing | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Colon, male | 39 | 25 | 64.1% |
| Colon, female | 35 | 22 | 62.9% |
| Rectum, male | 76 | 53 | 69.7% |
| Rectum, female | 58 | 36 | 62.1% |
| **Colorectal total** | **208** | **136** | **65.4%** |
| Stomach, male | 49 | 34 | 69.4% |
| Stomach, female | 20 | 15 | 75.0% |
| Oesophagus, male | 35 | 21 | 60.0% |
| Oesophagus, female | 18 | 12 | 66.7% |
| **GI total** | **330** | **218** | **66.1%** |

**Roughly 218 GI patients a year receive chemotherapy at MCC**, of whom 136 are colorectal.
Caveat: not every gastric or oesophageal regimen is fluoropyrimidine-based, though most are.
Colorectal first-line essentially always is.

Set against DPYD testing — 2 (2022-23), 8 (2023-24), 19 or 276 (2024-25):

- At **19**, DPYD covers about **8.7%** of the GI chemotherapy population. A real, measured,
  local gap.
- At **276**, testing *exceeds* the entire colorectal-plus-upper-GI chemotherapy population.
  That is only coherent if the figure counts assays rather than patients, or spans breast and
  haematology too. **This is evidence that 276 is not 276 patients**, and it makes 19 the more
  plausible patient count. Still needs confirming with MCC.
