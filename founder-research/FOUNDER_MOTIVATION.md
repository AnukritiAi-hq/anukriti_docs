## The story, in the founder's words (verbatim)

> "Even though I'm a Computer Science graduate, i'm someone who loves science
> at core very much especially genomics, space etc... Also while seeing the
> current ai advancements in software industry and its pace and comparing it
> to other industry i felt such advancements and that momentum should also be
> in sectors like this too which can make a huge impact, even in the law
> sector. That's how i get back into genomics did some research and got some
> case studies pressing this population wide genomic drug test as a issue and
> then went deeper and ended up here."

## Distilled narrative

**Background.** Abhimanyu is a Computer Science graduate with a deep,
long-standing personal love of science — genomics and space chief among his
interests. The technical foundation is software/AI; the pull is toward the
hard sciences.

**The spark.** Watching the *pace* of AI advancement in the software industry
and contrasting it with how little that same momentum has reached
high-impact, high-stakes sectors — healthcare, and even law. The conviction:
that velocity of progress *should* exist in fields where it can change lives,
not just ship features.

**The pivot.** That conviction sent him back into genomics deliberately. Not a
hobby return — a research-driven one. He ran his own research and gathered
case studies.

**The problem he found.** The case studies converged on a concrete,
under-addressed issue: **population-wide genomic drug testing**. Drug safety
and dosing are calibrated on genomically narrow (largely Eurocentric)
evidence, and applied uniformly across populations that metabolize drugs
differently. Going deeper into this gap is what produced Anukriti.

## Why this matters for positioning

- The motivation is **mission-first, not market-first** — the founder started
  from "this momentum should exist here" and a real biomedical inequity, then
  built the infrastructure. This is consistent with the platform's own
  framing in `anukriti-pgx-core/PLATFORM.md`: *equity in drug safety is an
  infrastructure problem, not a modeling problem.*
- The CS + genomics combination is the credibility story: software/AI fluency
  applied to a domain the founder genuinely cares about and has researched,
  rather than a generic "AI for X" play.
- The phrase **"population-wide genomic drug testing"** is the founder's own
  one-line articulation of the problem and maps directly to Anukriti's
  one-liner: *evidence-governed, population-aware pharmacogenomic reasoning.*

## Reusable framings derived from this

- **One-liner (origin flavor):** "A CS grad who loves genomics, frustrated
  that AI's pace hasn't reached the sectors where it matters most — so I went
  back into genomics, found that population-wide genomic drug testing is a
  real unsolved gap, and built Anukriti to close it."
- **Mission flavor:** "Bring software's velocity of progress to drug safety —
  for *every* population, not just the ones the evidence base happens to
  cover."
